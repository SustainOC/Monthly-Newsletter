Indoor Air Alert

The gas you cannot see or smell peaks in winter.

Radon is a natural radioactive gas that seeps up out of the ground and collects indoors. RDCO puts more than 30 per cent of homes in the Interior Health region above the Canadian guideline, which is a high share by national standards. It is the number one cause of lung cancer among people who have never smoked. Levels rise once the heating season starts and the windows stay shut, which is exactly why Health Canada says to test now. Kelowna, Penticton, Vernon and Salmon Arm all sit on the same geology.

From the Sustainability Office

The furnaces are on and the windows are shut. That is the whole story of November in the valley, and it quietly changes two things at once: what you spend on power, and what you are breathing while you spend it.

So this issue pairs the two. Heating is the biggest thing on a winter bill and the easiest to trim without noticing, and a sealed house is also a house where whatever is indoors stays indoors. That includes radon, which is why November is Radon Action Month and why the alert above is worth two minutes. There is a card each on the dark commute and on giving things that do not end up in a landfill by January.

One housekeeping note. The Teams group we flagged last month is up and running, and this issue is going out through it. If you have not joined yet, the link is at the bottom and it still takes one form. Here are five things worth doing this month.

5 Actions for November

What we can do, right now, across all four campuses.

1

Turn the Heat Down, Not Off

Heating is the biggest line on a winter bill, and most of it is spent warming rooms nobody is standing in. A setback costs nothing and you stop noticing it within a week.

  • →Drop the thermostat two degrees and wait a week.
  • →Set it back overnight and when the place is empty.
  • →Close doors and vents on rooms nobody uses.
  • →Put on a jumper before you touch the dial.
Local Tip

BC Hydro suggests 21°C when you are sitting still, 18°C while you are moving around, and 16°C overnight or when you are out. Every degree above 20°C adds roughly 5 per cent to the heating cost.

2

Unplug What Is Only Pretending to Be Off

Plenty of things in a home draw power around the clock while appearing to be switched off. November is when the dark evenings make the glowing standby lights obvious.

  • →Put the TV and console on one switched power bar.
  • →Unplug chargers once the device is full.
  • →Switch off the second fridge if it is half empty.
  • →Check the office: monitors and printers idle all night.
Local Tip

BC Hydro has put a set-top box and a games console, left plugged in, at about 36 dollars a year between them. On campus, the same logic applies to monitors and lab equipment left on over a long weekend.

3

Let the Air Actually Move

A tight, warm house is an efficient house right up to the point where nothing gets out. Cooking fumes, damp and radon all build up once the windows stop opening.

  • →Run the range hood every time you cook.
  • →Run the bathroom fan for 20 minutes after a shower.
  • →Crack a window briefly rather than all day.
  • →Report stale air or damp on campus to Facilities.
Local Tip

The Regional Air Quality Program is run jointly by RDCO, Kelowna, West Kelowna, Lake Country, Peachland and Westbank First Nation. It has advice on cutting wood smoke and idling, and points out that testing is the only way to know your radon level.

4

Winter-Proof the Trip to Campus

The trip that worked in September is a different trip in November. Dark by half four, ice on the Connector, and a bus that fills up the moment it rains.

  • →Fit proper winter tyres before the first freeze.
  • →Build in extra time rather than extra speed.
  • →Carry lights and something reflective after dark.
  • →Find a carpool match on the OC Commutes app.
Local Tip

Winter tyres or chains are required on BC's designated highways from October 1 to March 31, and to April 30 on high mountain passes. The fine is 121 dollars and two points. Tread under 3.5 mm no longer counts as a winter tyre.

5

Give Better, Bin Less

The run-up to the holidays is the single biggest waste spike of the year, and most of it is packaging and wrap that was never recyclable to begin with.

  • →Wrap in fabric, a gift bag or a box you already own.
  • →Give something second-hand, homemade or an experience.
  • →Keep foil wrap and ribbon out of the recycling cart.
  • →Save the tree for chipping instead of the landfill.
Local Tip

RDCO is blunt about it: foil wrapping paper is not recyclable. Nor are glass, Styrofoam, plastic bags, flexible plastics, electronics, batteries or light bulbs at the kerb. All of those are depot items in the Central Okanagan.

NOVEMBER CHALLENGE

One Action This Month: Turn It Down

Set your thermostat back for the rest of November and leave it there. Two degrees is enough to show up on a bill and not enough to make you cold. One dial, one month, and the habit carries you through to spring.

  • →Drop it two degrees and leave it alone for a week
  • →Set an overnight setback to 16°C while you sleep
  • →Turn it down whenever the place is empty
  • →Shut the door on a room nobody is using

RDCO gave away 285 free radon test kits this autumn and they are all gone, with pickup in early November and results due back by the end of February. If you missed out, kits can be bought from the BC Lung Foundation or Take Action on Radon.

Chip the Tree, Do Not Chuck It
